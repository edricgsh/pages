Deadweight Loss — The Textbook View

Classical economics: trade liberalization is most efficient. Tariffs create two "deadweight loss" triangles — lost welfare nobody recovers. First-year economics says tariffs are always bad.

But real-world trade policy is more nuanced. Tariffs have additional effects the simple model misses...

Why Tariffs Exist Despite Deadweight Loss

🏭 Infant Industry Protection
  • Hamilton's 18th-century argument
  • US (19th C): tariffs >40% → steel output ↑500×
  • China: car tariffs >200% nurtured domestic auto
  • India: "Make in India" via high tariffs
🤝 Negotiation Leverage
  • Tariffs damage opponents → bargaining chip
  • Trump: NAFTA renegotiation via tariff threats
  • Canada retaliated on swing-state goods (ketchup, orange juice, toilet paper)
🗳️ Political Appeal
  • "Protect jobs" resonates during downturns
  • Swing-state targeting: Pennsylvania steel unions
  • Biden & Trump both blocked Japan Steel's US Steel deal
⚔️ Retaliation Risk
  • 1963 Chicken-Truck War
  • Trade wars escalate unpredictably
  • Consumers on both sides pay

2. The Tariff Game Prisoner's Dilemma

Trade between two countries becomes a prisoner's dilemma. Each chooses: Open (free trade) or Protect (tariffs).

Country B: Open
Country B: Protect
Country A: Open
A: +3, B: +3
A: -5, B: +8
Country A: Protect
A: +8, B: -5
A: -3, B: -3

Nash equilibrium: Both protect (mutual loss). In repeated games, tit-for-tat (start open, mirror opponent's move) breaks the trap. Key: the threat must be communicated.

🔄 Tit-for-Tat
  • Start open, warn retaliation
  • Mirror opponent's last move
  • Make your threat credible
🤝 Institutions
  • WTO (166 members, 98% of world trade)
  • EU: extreme interest alignment
  • NAFTA/USMCA: regional agreements

3. 300 Years of Global Trade Timeline

1700s–1800s

Mercantilism. Britain's avg tariff ~50%. Colonies exist for the empire.

1817

Ricardo's Comparative Advantage. Free trade cornerstone. Britain lowers tariffs.

1800s

US & Germany ignore free trade. Hamilton's infant-industry argument. Tariffs >40%. Steel ↑500× in 30 years. US overtakes Britain.

1930

Smoot-Hawley Tariff Act. US tariffs 20–60% on 20,000+ goods. Europe retaliates. US imports/exports ↓66%. Great Depression deepens.

1947

GATT signed. Post-WWII tariff reductions. Avg: 22% (1947) → 5% (2000). Exports: 5% → 25% of global GDP.

2018–Now

New protectionism. US-China trade war, Brexit, pandemic nationalism. WTO paralyzed. Deglobalization trend.

These always balance. The US trade deficit is mirrored by America's financial account surplus.

The Circular Flow

1️⃣ Countries earn USD from trade surpluses
2️⃣ Central banks accumulate USD reserves → print local currency
3️⃣ Reserves reinvested into US assets (Treasuries, stocks, MBS)
4️⃣ Capital inflow drives up USD exchange rate
5️⃣ Strong dollar makes US exports expensive, imports cheap → deficit widens
6️⃣ Repeat for 50+ years

Why Doesn't the Dollar Depreciate to Fix It?

  • 🌍 Global demand for USD is structural — it's the world's reserve currency
  • 🏦 Central banks peg/manage currencies, suppressing automatic adjustment
  • 📈 "Better economy = bigger deficit" — strong economy attracts investment → dollar appreciates → deficit grows

5. The Triffin Dilemma Root Cause

"A country that issues the world's reserve currency will inevitably run a trade deficit."

— Robert Triffin, 1960s (predicted the 50+ year US deficit before it happened)

The dollar's reserve currency status means the US must supply USD to the world by running a trade deficit — effectively printing money to buy goods.

The Fed admits this. Most media & politicians show only half the picture.

Is the Trade Deficit Bad? → It depends.

🟢 Healthy
  • Australia: decades of deficit driven by strong investment demand
  • Fast-growing economies attract capital naturally
🔴 Unhealthy
  • Pre-2010 Eurozone: Greece/Spain overheated, borrowing too much
  • Sri Lanka: currency collapse + foreign debt → goods shortage

Key shift: Economic growth is no longer #1. Trump sacrifices growth to achieve other goals — a radical departure from decades of US policy.

Milan's Two-Pronged Strategy

🌐 Multilateral: Mar-a-Lago Accord

Goal: Devalue USD with allies' help

1️⃣ Countries help sell USD → weaker dollar
2️⃣ Roll Treasuries into 50–100 year bonds → lower long-term rates
3️⃣ Fed provides USD swap lines

Problem: Disguised debt default. Hedgies arbitrage it. Near-impossible.

🇺🇸 Unilateral: Charge for Treasury Access

Goal: Revenue + discourage USD holdings

Charge foreign institutions a "usage fee" to hold US Treasuries → reduces dollar attractiveness.

Problem: Everyone sells → rates spike → govt debt costs explode.

Tariffs As a Negotiation Tool

The Milan Formula:

  1. Impose very high tariffs on everyone (shock value)
  2. Offer to lower them in exchange for:
    • Cooperating on tariffs against China
    • Investing in the US / writing a check
    • Giving up security umbrella demands
  3. If no deal → tariffs stay, generating revenue + protecting manufacturing

Think of it like haggling: merchant asks $1,000 knowing cost is $50. Both know the number is ridiculous — that's the game.

But sovereign nations don't have this "industry rule." The April 2 Excel tariff list crashed the bond market (10-year yield spiked 60bp in a week). He suspended tariffs for 90 days.

7. The Contradictions Why It Might Fail

Tariff Paradox #1

Tariffs raise prices → Fed hikes rates → govt debt costs explode → contradicts #1 goal.

Tariff Paradox #2

Milan's "currency offset" theory: tariffs make USD appreciate. But that makes manufacturing worse.

Dollar Paradox

US wants allies to sell USD (weaker dollar) but maintain dollar supremacy. Can't have both.

Fed Paradox

Trump wants low rates. Fed wants to raise to fight tariff inflation. Irreconcilable.

China Alignment

Trump wants manufacturing reshoring + rate cuts. China also wants US rate cuts + domestic consumption. Adversaries, aligned incentives.

Execution Paradox

Shock-and-retreat destroyed trust in Treasuries as "risk-free." Now there's a Trump Risk Premium.

The Ironic Takeaway

Trump's policies aim to weaken the dollar, cut rates, bring manufacturing home. But he needs the Fed's cooperation — and he's undermining Fed independence. The tariff chaos has created a "Trump risk premium," making his core fiscal goal harder to achieve.

As 小Lin说 puts it: "Like an obstacle race where you planned the route, but slipped at the first step and fell into the water."

The Bottom Line

Tariffs are a double-edged sword. Targeted protection can nurture infant industries (US 1800s, China 1990s). Blanket tariffs risk retaliation spirals and deadweight loss.

The US trade deficit is not a bug — it's a feature of dollar reserve status (Triffin Dilemma). Tariffs on individual countries just shift import sources; they can't fix the structural deficit.

Trump's playbook is not crazy — it's radical. Milan's logic is consistent: use tariff shock + security threats as leverage. But contradictions are deep: tariffs inflate → Fed raises → debt costs spike.

The real question: Is the US willing to sacrifice dollar hegemony to save manufacturing? Milan's answer, buried under denial, appears to be yes — the most consequential shift since Bretton Woods.
