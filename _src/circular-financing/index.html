AI Circular Financing — The $800B Money Loop

How chipmakers and cloud providers invest in their own customers — and the bubble risk nobody wants to talk about

Key Deals (2025-2026)

  • NVIDIA → OpenAI: $30B equity stake; OpenAI commits to NVIDIA GPUs
  • Google → Anthropic: Up to $40B ($10B cash + 5GW TPU cloud)
  • Amazon → Anthropic: $100B compute commitment (5GW)
  • Microsoft → OpenAI: Multi-billion Azure compute deal + equity
  • AMD → Anthropic / Meta: Equity + Instinct GPU purchase commitments
  • NVIDIA → Neoclouds: $20B+ to CoreWeave, Lambda Labs, etc.

The Circularity Problem

  • Supplier invests → AI company buys supplier's product → Supplier books as revenue
  • Same dollar flows in a loop — inflating both sides of the P&L
  • Estimated $800B+ in circular commitments across the AI industry
  • NVIDIA alone has $40B+ in AI equity bets (2025-2026)
  • Analysts warn: echoes Lucent/Nortel vendor financing in the telecom bubble

What's Different This Time?

  • Real demand exists: Enterprise AI adoption is accelerating
  • Revenue outside the loop: ChatGPT, Claude, and API sales generate genuine revenue
  • Compute is a real asset: GPUs hold resale value; fiber optics in 2000 did not
  • Hyperscaler competition: Google, Microsoft, AWS all need AI to drive cloud growth
  • But if enterprise demand stalls, the circular floor drops out

How It Works

Circular financing is a closed-loop funding arrangement where a supplier (chipmaker, cloud provider) invests capital in a customer (AI lab, startup), and that customer uses the capital to purchase the supplier's own products or services. The money flows in a loop: supplier → investment → customer → purchase → supplier books as revenue.

This isn't new — it resembles vendor financing from the dot-com era, where Lucent and Nortel lent money to telecoms that then bought their networking gear. The scale, however, is unprecedented: ~$800 billion in estimated circular commitments across the AI supply chain as of mid-2026.

Why It Matters

The risk is that a meaningful portion of AI industry "revenue" is actually just capital cycling in a closed loop — not real economic activity from end customers. If enterprise AI adoption slows:

1. AI labs can't justify their compute burn rates without external revenue
2. Suppliers' "customer revenue" evaporates when their own investments dry up
3. The neocloud layer (financed by supplier equity) defaults on GPU-backed debt
4. A 2001-style write-down cascade as over-leveraged compute assets get repriced

On the other hand, defenders argue that real AI product adoption (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, enterprise agents) is generating measurable revenue from actual end customers — the circular financing is just accelerating an otherwise real trend, not fabricating one.

⚠ The $800B Question

Analysts at Carus Signal, Tom Tunguz, and others have drawn comparisons to Nortel's vendor financing in the late 1990s. Nortel lent $billions to telcos to buy its equipment — when the dot-com bubble burst, those telcos defaulted, Nortel ate the losses, and the stock collapsed from $124 to $0.47. NVIDIA's $40B+ equity portfolio, $110B in GPU-backed vendor financing, and the $800B industry circular loop are the modern analogue — with the crucial difference that NVIDIA's technology is genuinely transformative and demand is real. The question is: how much of the revenue is real vs. manufactured by the loop?
