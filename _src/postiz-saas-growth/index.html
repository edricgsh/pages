I Grew My SaaS From $17K to $143K MRR in 4 Months

Nevo (Postiz) on Starter Story — the agentic pivot, the OpenClaw trend, viral X articles, and how churn dropped from 26% to 13.7%.

AGENTIC SAAS GROWTH MARKETING 16 MIN

◈ CORE THESIS

Build before the trend, then ride it with marketing. Postiz spent 1.5 years getting to $21K MRR as a social scheduler, then pivoted to agentic (AI-agent-ready) SaaS, caught the OpenClaw wave on X, and hit $143K MRR in 4 months (~$1K MRR/day). The agentic pivot was the vehicle — marketing was the gas.

The hockey stick, the viral cascade, and the playbook — build before the trend, go agentic, then market like crazy.

THE STORY $17K → $143K MRR

The Numbers 0:00

  • Timeline: 1.5 years to reach $21K MRR, then 4 months to $143K MRR — growing ~$1K MRR every day.
  • Monthly progression: Feb $41K → Mar $76K → Apr $80K → May $109K → late June $140K+.
  • Subscribers: 4,271 (at time of recording).
  • Churn: dropped from 26–28% to 13.7% after the agentic pivot.
  • 4,271 subscribers + 13.7% churn at $143K MRR → that's the "true hockey stick" Pat calls out on camera.

The Pivot — From Scheduler to Agentic SaaS 1:42

Postiz was a social media scheduling tool for 30+ platforms. Nevo completely pivoted it to an agentic social media scheduling tool — instead of clicking to schedule, you do it via agent tools: Claude, OpenClaw (OpenClaw/Claude's agent framework), ChatGPT, etc.

  • The trigger: "A few months ago there was a nice guy named Peter that released a tool called OpenClaw… I'm a hype guy. If I see some hype, I always try to understand if I can somehow use it for Postiz."
  • He "leaned into OpenClaw and put all the tools out there to make it agentic ready."

The Viral Cascade — One Article Changed Everything 3:06

  • Oliver Henry's article on X: he was making money for his app (Snugly) on TikTok "100% automatically with OpenClaw and Postiz." The article got 7.2 million views → brought Postiz 700 concurrent trials every day, non-stop.
  • Second article: another 1.5M views → MRR started growing every day.
  • Nevo reverse-engineered it: wrote his own OpenClaw + Postiz article with 200 followers → 500K views. Realized X was pushing articles (people spend more time reading them than posts).
  • Paid reposters: he checks "view activity" on viral creators' posts, and influencers reach out — "can I book you for a paid promotion repost?" $60–$350 per repost; every article he publishes gets ~20 reposters amplifying it.

"Every time I have an article with Mud Creator, I have like 20 other reposters reposting the article. It's just insane what you can do with a little bit of growth hacks."

Why Agentic Kills Churn 8:32

Pat's insight: "Churn is typically caused by some human who decides not to do that human thing anymore. Now the AI agents are doing it automatically, every single day, without any intervention. So the churn's less."

Before: users had to log in daily, click, write, iterate with AI — a time sink they eventually quit. Now: if they have any content source, they "feed it to the LLM and create an entire week of marketing stuff every day basically."

THE PLAYBOOK Nevo's Advice for Builders

1. Make Your SaaS Agent-Ready 7:07

  • Almost any SaaS should have an API. Take your public API, create an .md file describing it, and make it ready for agents (MCP-style).
  • List it everywhere: "You can go to Claude and ChatGPT marketplaces and list your MD file there. That's exactly what I did and it brought me so much traffic."

2. Build Before the Trend 7:34

"You should build before and then wait for the trend to happen — and not build when the trend happens. If you build right now and wait for the thing to happen, compared to doing it when it happens, then somebody else is probably going to eat the cake."

3. The Agentic Market Is Early Adopters 7:51

"The thing about agentic is that it's actually not that big of a market. It's not the boomer audience. It's a really, really early adopter market." — the audience is small but highly engaged and technical; they're the ones writing the viral articles.

4. Make Your App Reliable 9:47

"You need to make your app super reliable. I at some point stopped making new features. If there's a new feature, there's going to be bugs. I want the app to be perfect. I have 30 social media channels — I need all of them to work. I can't focus on new stuff before I fix the old stuff."

"The apps that are going to win are the ones that don't break. Agents will somehow know which are the reliable ones and which are not."

What the Money Changed 8:54

  • Marketing budget: at $17K MRR he couldn't hire anybody. Now he can book influencers for $60–$350 quote reposts at scale.
  • First hires: two people — one for support, one for development. "With $17K MRR you can't really hire anybody."

THE COMMENTARY Pat & Gus — Why This Actually Worked

The "I Should Have Done It" Lesson 11:45

Gus: "I remember so many times sitting there thinking, you know what I should do? I should write an article. And I just didn't. It's so easy to say 'oh, he got lucky.' But the thing that doesn't make it happen is you just don't do it."

The Vehicle vs. The Gas 12:58

"We can sit here and talk about building products agentically — that's pretty straightforward, you could ChatGPT your way into it. But it's probably not going to drive revenue because you don't have these two things he had:

  • 1. Marketing: He saw the X articles and didn't just build — he did more X articles, more marketing. "The agentic thing is the vehicle. That vehicle would have had no gas in it if he didn't do the marketing."
  • 2. An existing product solving a problem: "Trend-hoppers say 'I'm just going to build an agentic thing' — but they don't have anything yet. He had a product out in the world solving a real problem (social media scheduling), which allowed him to create this insane business story."

The Future — Agent Payments 10:42

Nevo's bet: agents will soon complete purchases themselves. Today there's "human in the loop" — even with a CLI, a Stripe checkout page pops up for the founder. When agent payments land, the whole flow becomes automated.

Pat's framing: when agents decide which tools to use, "his product will be recommended through LLMs for agents to use — and that could be where the business goes to a million dollars a month if he captures that trend." Right now it's "the wild west."
